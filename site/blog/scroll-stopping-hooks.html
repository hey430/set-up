The First 3 Seconds: How to Write Hooks That Stop the Scroll

  • Aug 28, 2026
  • 2 min read
  • Haul House Team

A hook is whatever happens in the first one to three seconds of a video that makes a viewer stop scrolling. In short-form feeds it's the most important creative decision you make. A great product with a weak opening never gets the chance to sell.

Every script we write at Haul House starts with the hook, and we usually write several per video. Here's how we think about them.

A hook has three layers

A strong hook works on more than one level at the same moment:

  • Visual hook: what the viewer sees first, like motion, an unexpected action, a close-up or a dramatic before/after.
  • Text hook: the on-screen caption, which matters for viewers watching with the sound off.
  • Verbal hook: the first line the creator says.

When all three point at the same idea, the hook lands. When they compete, it muddles.

Seven hook types that work for products

1. The call-out

Speak directly to the person the product is for. "If you meal-prep on Sundays, you need to see this."

2. The bold claim

Lead with the outcome, but only if you can prove it on camera. "This is the only pasta my kids finish."

3. The problem

Open on the frustration. "I was so tired of my hair looking flat by noon…"

4. The curiosity gap

Give the viewer a reason to wait for the answer. "I didn't expect the $12 one to win."

5. The demo-first

Skip the talking and open on the most satisfying visual moment: the pour, the peel, the transformation.

6. The social proof

Borrow momentum. "This keeps selling out on TikTok Shop, so I finally tried it."

7. The haul

The format we're named after. "Everything I bought from TikTok Shop this month, ranked." Hauls make the viewer want to see the next item.

A hook earns attention. The rest of the video has to pay it off. A hook that over-promises gets the view but loses the sale.

Adjusting hooks by platform

  • TikTok and TikTok Shop: sound-on, conversational and trend-aware. Creator personality is the hook.
  • Meta ads: many people watch with the sound off, so the text and visual hooks carry more of the load. Make the benefit readable at a glance.
  • Instagram Reels: aesthetics matter more. Open on a beautiful, clearly composed first frame.

How we test hooks

Hooks are the cheapest variable to test and the one with the most upside. Our approach:

  1. Write several hooks per concept, drawing on different hook types.
  2. Keep the body the same and swap only the first few seconds, so you're testing one variable.
  3. Watch retention, not just clicks. Early drop-off tells you whether the hook worked. Later drop-off points to the body.
  4. Feed winners back into briefs for creators and affiliates, so good hooks spread across the whole program.

Hook checklist

  • Does something happen in the very first frame?
  • Would it make sense with the sound off?
  • Is it clear who this is for?
  • Does the rest of the video deliver on it?
  • Does it sound like the creator, not the brand?

Hooks are the front door; UGC and affiliate creators are how you get them in front of buyers at scale. If you want scripts built around tested hooks, let's talk.
